Independent AI review of the Silicoase licensing draft
Reviewed commit: 9a9f992
Date: 2026-10-04

This was a separate reviewer, with read-only access. This is a textual licensing
review, not a legal opinion. The draft generally implements noncommercial,
source-available terms, but the following issues should be resolved before
publication. Line numbers refer to LICENSE in the reviewed commit.

High priority

1. Licensor identity and ownership (lines 2, 14-16, 64-71): "Silicoase" is not
identified as a legal entity. Git authorship does not establish company ownership.
Founder, employee, contractor, and contributor rights need verification. The
license preserves contributors' ownership without granting Silicoase commercial
relicensing rights. A reviewed contribution agreement or individual permissions
would be needed for those rights.
Source: https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap2.html (sections 201 and 204).

2. Downstream grants for modifications (lines 14-16, 35-36, 44, 70-71): only
Silicoase makes the operative grant, although modifiers retain their copyrights.
Define contributors and expressly establish their downstream licensing grants;
do not imply their copyrights belong to Silicoase.

3. Permitted research (lines 18-26): the commercial definition may exclude
industry-sponsored research, commercialization-linked academic work, paid
research services, fee-based education, and cost-recovery hosting. Decide which
activities should be permitted and state those permissions in operative terms.
Salary-supported research is not clearly prohibited merely because someone is
paid, but there is no clear safe harbor. The Creative Commons discussion is a
useful comparison, not an interpretation governing this custom license.
Source: https://wiki.creativecommons.org/wiki/NonCommercial_interpretation

Other consequential findings

4. Network disclosure (lines 42-52): only modified hosted versions trigger it;
independent frontends and indirect access create ambiguity. Internal colleagues
using a private modified research server are also remote users. Define the
trigger, internal-use treatment, version correspondence, and actual download
mechanism. Commercial hosting remains prohibited separately.
Comparator: https://www.gnu.org/licenses/agpl-3.0.de.html (section 13).

5. Closed-source limits (lines 47-52): private noncommercial modifications may
remain private; independent wrappers need not disclose their source. Disclosure
is to recipients/users, not necessarily the entire public. Decide whether those
limits match the intended policy. Copyright does not protect research ideas or
methods against independent reimplementation.
Source: https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap1.html (sections 102 and 103).

6. Branding (lines 56-62): the embedded logo is part of covered code, but the
relationship between copyright permission and trademark restrictions is unclear.
State whether modified interfaces must remove branding and identify assets and
marks. Verify actual ownership and geographic protection; the clause creates no
trademark rights by itself.
Source: https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/basics/why-register-your-trademark

7. Provenance and enforceability: audit dependencies, copied material, logo
assets, external contributions, and human authorship of AI-assisted work. A
copyright notice does not establish protectability. Counsel should assess assent,
conditions versus covenants, remedies, and jurisdictions. These are questions,
not findings that the license is invalid.
Sources: https://www.copyright.gov/newsnet/2025/1060.html
https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2011/02/17/09-15932.pdf

8. Cure (lines 80-84): explicitly address self-discovered violations corrected
before notice, automatic reinstatement, and subsequent violations.

README substantially matches the terms; packaging metadata correctly uses a
custom LicenseRef and includes LICENSE. No license changes were made as part
of this review. Policy decisions and counsel review remain outstanding.
