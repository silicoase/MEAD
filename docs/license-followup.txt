Assessment of the independent review

The eight findings are well founded as drafting concerns or decisions to resolve.
They do not establish that the license is invalid. Sections 2 and 3 already
prohibit commercial use and source-withholding distribution, including commercial
internal R&D, without requiring that software be sold. Section 4 already limits
misleading branding. These are useful foundations for the stated goals.

The most consequential unresolved issues are legal identity/authority, explicit
licensing of downstream modifications, and the funded-research boundary. Network
disclosure, branding treatment, and cure need more precise mechanics. Private
noncommercial modifications, independent programs, and disclosure only to actual
recipients/users are existing policy limits, not accidental drafting failures.

Two qualifications to the preserved review:

* Section 204's signed-writing requirement concerns copyright ownership transfers
  (including exclusive licenses), not every nonexclusive permission. Section 101
  expressly excludes nonexclusive licenses from that definition. An assignment
  and a sufficient commercial relicensing permission are different approaches.
  Sources: [Copyright ownership, sections 201 and 204](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap2.html),
  [Definitions, section 101](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap1.html).
* The modified-hosting trigger resembles AGPL section 13, but this custom license
  must define its own effect. GNU pages were unavailable during this follow-up;
  this report does not claim to have independently verified that comparator.

Fixable drafting issues: proposed language for counsel review

1. Identify the grantors and license downstream additions explicitly.

After confirming identity, define "Licensor" as [full legal person/entity name],
using Silicoase as the brand/trading name if accurate. Do not substitute a company
name or alter the copyright notice until ownership is confirmed.

Add to scope:

  "Covered Modifications" means modifications and derivative works of the
  Software covered by this license. "Contributor" means a person or entity
  that holds copyright in material it distributes or makes available as part
  of the Software or Covered Modifications under this license. "You" means
  the person or entity exercising permissions under this license.

Replace the section 2 grant with:

  The Licensor and each Contributor grant you, to the extent of their authority
  over their respective contributions, a worldwide, royalty-free, nonexclusive
  copyright license to reproduce, modify, distribute, publicly display, and
  publicly perform the Software and Covered Modifications, and to use and
  study them, solely for purposes permitted by section 2 and provided that
  you comply with this license's applicable conditions.

Add to section 3:

  When you distribute or make available Covered Modifications under this
  license, you must license your copyright in those modifications to each
  recipient and network user under the grant in section 2. Each recipient
  receives permissions directly from the applicable copyright holders. You
  must have authority to grant those permissions. You may not impose terms
  on covered material that prevent recipients from exercising permissions
  granted by this license.

This does not give Silicoase commercial relicensing rights in others' code.
Upstream submission terms need a separate decision and contribution agreement
if that ability is wanted. Third-party dependencies retain their own terms.
Statutory background: [Ownership and transfers](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap2.html).

2. Make source delivery verifiable without silently changing its reach.

Add to the corresponding-source definition:

  Corresponding source must match the version distributed or operated,
  including its Covered Modifications and the covered templates and assets
  needed to reproduce it. Source must be supplied in a form recipients can
  download and retain. For distribution, provide it with the copy or provide
  a working source-download location with that copy at the time of delivery.

Replace the modified-network paragraph, preserving its existing basic scope:

  If you operate a modified version of the Software and allow any other person
  to interact with it remotely over a network, directly or through an interface
  or intermediary service you provide, prominently provide those users a
  working link to download and retain that version's complete corresponding
  source under this license at no additional charge. The link must remain
  available throughout operation of that version for those users. An unrelated
  upstream version or an incomplete patch alone does not satisfy this duty.

State explicitly whether employees and collaborators count as "other persons".
The current text has no institutional/internal-network exemption. Adding one
would change policy, not merely clarify punctuation. Indirect interaction above
addresses offered functionality, not passive receipt of published research
findings. Independent interface code stays outside corresponding source unless
it is itself a Covered Modification.

3. Clarify branding assets and the notices/branding distinction.

Identify the inline SVG in src/mad/templates/review.html (currently lines 18-24)
as a Silicoase brand asset after confirming its provenance. Distinguish legal
attribution, interface branding, and copyright in the asset itself. Suggested
addition consistent with reserving fork branding:

  The section 2 grant permits copying embedded Silicoase brand assets as part
  of complete source copies and displaying them in an otherwise unmodified
  MAD interface. It does not permit extracting, altering, or using those
  assets to identify another product or service. Modified interfaces must
  remove Silicoase product branding unless separately authorized in writing;
  truthful textual attribution and legal notices must be retained. Removing
  branding solely to comply with this section is permitted. Statutory rights
  to use marks remain unaffected.

Mandatory removal is a policy choice requiring confirmation; alternatively allow
the original logo with a prominent "modified/unofficial" notice. Neither choice
should require removal of copyright notices. The clause cannot create trademark
ownership or eliminate statutory defenses. [USPTO trademark rights](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/basics/why-register-your-trademark).

4. Express first-violation cure and later-violation treatment.

To preserve the present first-violation policy, replace section 6 with:

  Your permissions terminate upon violation of an applicable condition of
  this license. For your first violation, permissions are automatically
  reinstated when you cease the violation and fully cure it, provided you
  complete the cure before receiving written notice or within 30 days after
  receiving written notice from an affected copyright holder. Subsequent
  violations do not receive automatic reinstatement; each affected copyright
  holder may reinstate permissions under its rights in writing. Reinstatement
  does not waive liability or obligations arising before cure. Complying
  downstream recipients retain their own permissions.

Counsel should decide how simultaneous violations and multiple copyright holders
affect termination and cure. A more generous discovery-based cure for every
violation is an alternative policy, not a necessary correction. "Fully cure"
requires a practical explanation for completed distributions and missed source
offers; simply stopping use may not remedy past noncompliance.

Additional concrete issue: exported HTML reports

src/mad/review.py, lines 329-331, copies the template into review.html and inserts
the run payload. That template includes MAD JavaScript/CSS and the inline logo.
Thus the generated report contains covered software/branding even though raw
results are excluded by section 5. Sharing the report can require LICENSE,
attribution and corresponding source. The template currently lacks the full
license text and an explicit copyright notice. This is a publication usability
issue worth fixing after deciding the policy for these reports.

Options: (a) retain current coverage and embed notices plus complete corresponding
source/a version-matched download with every export; or (b) expressly authorize
sharing unmodified generated reports, with their embedded viewer and logo, under
a narrowly defined exception. Decide whether commercial dissemination of results
is allowed when the underlying MAD use was permitted. Do not accidentally exempt
commercial operation of MAD or relicensing the extracted viewer. Data payloads
may separately contain copied code or third-party material. No exemption is
assumed or applied here.

Policy decisions requiring the human user's answers

* Who holds copyright and branding rights? Who can sign written agreements?
  Git authorship, a brand name, and a copyright notice do not establish title.
* Salary/grant-funded academic work: should payment for research labor alone be
  permissible? If yes, add a safe harbor, for example: "Salary, scholarships,
  and noncommercial research grants do not by themselves make an otherwise
  permitted research use commercial." This does not decide industry sponsorship.
* Industry-sponsored work, commercial sponsor deliverables/exclusive rights,
  tuition-funded teaching, cost-recovery services, and internal company research:
  resolve each explicitly. Any allowed exception should say it overrides the
  otherwise applicable commercial-purpose examples. An academic label alone
  is inadequate. CC's discussion is a useful analogy, not governing law or an
  interpretation incorporated into this custom license.
  [CC noncommercial interpretation](https://wiki.creativecommons.org/wiki/NonCommercial_interpretation).
* Does "prevent closed-source reuse" mean the current recipient/user disclosure
  rule, public disclosure, or disclosure even for private modifications? Current
  terms allow private noncommercial work and exclude independent wrappers.
* Must modified interfaces remove the logo, or may they retain it with a clear
  unofficial-release notice? How should generated reports be shared?
* Do accepted upstream contributions need alternative-license permissions?
  Contributor ownership can remain intact with suitable separate permissions.
* Is withholding all patent permission intentional? A copyright grant does not
  resolve patent exposure. A limited contributor patent grant is a separate choice.

User-confirmed decisions and resulting proposed wording

The user identified the legal rights holder/licensor as Silicoase, Inc. Confirmed
policy: permit salary/grant-funded noncommercial academic work; require written
agreements for industry-sponsored research, paid services, and company uses.
The user also asked whether noncommercial hobby use is permitted: yes, the
existing broad noncommercial grant permits it. The academic safe harbor should
not narrow that grant to academic users only.

Use "Silicoase, Inc. (the Licensor)" in the scope definition and operative
agreement provisions. Identify the corporation in its copyright notice for
material it owns; retain applicable notices for others' material. Keep Silicoase
as the brand name in the branding clause. Corporate identity confirmation does
not substitute for inspecting actual assignments and contributor permissions.

Proposed replacement for the section 2 purpose definition, to accompany the
grant proposed above:

  A noncommercial purpose is one that is neither intended for nor directed
  toward commercial advantage or monetary compensation. Permitted purposes
  include personal hobby projects and noncommercial academic research.
  Ordinary salaries, stipends, scholarships, and non-industry research grants
  supporting otherwise noncommercial academic research do not, by themselves,
  make that research commercial.

  Regardless of an organization's nonprofit or educational status, a separate
  written agreement with the Licensor is required for industry-sponsored
  research; use by or on behalf of a for-profit company, including internal
  research and development; selling or licensing the Software or Covered
  Modifications; providing paid hosting, consulting, research, teaching, or
  other services using them, including services charged at cost; or incorporating
  them into a commercial product or service. These requirements apply even
  when no Software copy is sold or distributed and when source is provided.

Counsel should define industry sponsorship and company-controlled nonprofit
arrangements precisely. Paid teaching and cost-recovery services are included
in this proposed agreement-required category consistent with the selected
paid-service policy; ordinary salaried research labor is treated separately.
Noncommercial personal use by someone who happens to work at a company remains
permitted when undertaken personally, not for or on behalf of that company.

Counsel questions and release preparation

Verify employee scope of employment, contractor agreements, external contributions,
logo origin, dependency notices/compatibility, and sufficient human authorship.
AI assistance does not categorically defeat copyright; prompting alone does not
establish authorship in generated expression. [Copyright Office AI report summary](https://www.copyright.gov/newsnet/2025/1060.html).

Assess assent and contract formation, statutory-rights nexus, remedies, applicable
jurisdictions, warranty/liability limitations, and notice delivery. Drafting grants
as conditional helps communicate scope but cannot guarantee infringement remedies
for every restriction. Jacobsen enforced conditions of the Artistic License;
MDY distinguishes license conditions from covenants and requires an implicated
exclusive right for infringement. These do not decide this license's enforceability.
[Jacobsen v. Katzer](https://www.cafc.uscourts.gov/opinions-orders/08-1001.pdf),
[MDY v. Blizzard, pp. 12-15](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2011/02/17/09-15932.pdf).

Copyright does not grant control over independently implemented research ideas
or methods. Restrictions on MAD code are not exclusive ownership of the research
concept. [17 USC 102(b), 103(b)](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap1.html).

README is consistent with the present license. Metadata declares a custom
LicenseRef and includes LICENSE; no built artifact was inspected in this review.
After settling terms, align README and package metadata, version the revised
license rather than silently repurposing an already published version, and verify
license inclusion in wheel and source distribution. Earlier authorized grants
cannot simply be presumed revoked by publishing new text.

Web research: established templates and generators (2026-10-04)

Conclusion: the reviewed candidates provide established building blocks, but no
unchanged template found in this search implements all confirmed MAD policies.
This is a search result, not a claim that no such license exists anywhere.

PolyForm Noncommercial 1.0.0 is the closest software-specific starting point.
It expressly allows hobby use and broadly permits educational/nonprofit use
regardless of funding obligations. It lacks mandatory corresponding-source and
network-disclosure clauses. These differ from MAD's industry-funding restriction
and source-sharing goals. It also grants patent permission, unlike MAD's draft.
[Official text](https://polyformproject.org/licenses/noncommercial/1.0.0).

PolyForm describes its project as lawyers and technologists developing standard
licenses. Its license-text reuse permission requires removal of PolyForm and
polyformproject.org references if the text is modified. A tailored version must
be identified as custom, not as unchanged PolyForm. SPDX inclusion provides an
identifier, not a legal certification or open-source approval.
[Project and modification terms](https://github.com/polyformproject/polyform-licenses/blob/1.0.0/README.md),
[SPDX entry](https://spdx.org/licenses/PolyForm-Noncommercial-1.0.0.html).

AGPLv3 supplies established source-disclosure mechanics, including modified
network services, but permits commercial activity. AGPL plus an alternative
commercial license does not require every commercial user to pay: users may
choose and comply with AGPL. A noncommercial restriction added to it would not
be standard AGPL. GNU pages could not be fetched; the full license was available
in GitHub's Choose a License catalog, including sections 2, 4, 7 and 13.
[AGPL text and summary](https://choosealicense.com/licenses/agpl-3.0/).

CC BY-NC-SA looks attractive for noncommercial/share-alike content, but CC
recommends against its licenses for software, including because they lack
software source-distribution provisions. Its chooser is not a substitute for
a software license with MAD's requirements.
[CC software FAQ](https://creativecommons.org/faq/#can-i-apply-a-creative-commons-license-to-software).

Other candidates do not solve the stated policy:
* Prosperity permits a commercial trial and broadly exempts nonprofit/educational
  use, without MAD's source-disclosure duties.
  [Prosperity 3.0.0 text](https://blog.licensezero.com/2019/12/15/prosperity-3.html).
* Business Source License 1.1 permits nonproduction use and transitions to an
  open-source license no later than four years after first public distribution.
  That differs from requiring permission for commercial research indefinitely.
  [BSL official text](https://mariadb.com/bsl11/).
* Functional Source License is a noncompetition approach with two-year eventual
  open-source conversion, not a continuing noncommercial restriction.
  [Fair Source license guide](https://fair.io/licenses/).
* Elastic License 2.0 allows internal company use and many commercial applications;
  its principal restrictions concern managed services, license keys and notices.
  [Elastic FAQ](https://www.elastic.co/licensing/elastic-license/faq/).

Tools found:
* GitHub's license-template picker and Choose a License help apply existing
  open-source licenses. They do not assemble or validate the selected MAD policy.
  [GitHub instructions](https://docs.github.com/en/communities/setting-up-your-project-for-healthy-contributions/adding-a-license-to-a-repository),
  [Chooser](https://choosealicense.com/).
* generate-license inserts licenses supported by Choose a License from a CLI;
  this is template automation rather than legal drafting. It was inspected as
  an example, not installed or evaluated for maintenance/security.
  [Generator repository](https://github.com/generate/generate-license).

Recommended next step: decide whether strict industry-funding restrictions and
source-sharing obligations remain essential. If they do, use established texts
as drafting references for a short custom, counsel-reviewed license and separate
branding terms. A PolyForm-derived draft is a reasonable candidate, not an
automatic endorsement. If policy is relaxed enough to adopt unchanged PolyForm,
it offers a standard software noncommercial template but loses the protections
identified above. No operative license was replaced during this search.

Implementation record: Version 2.0 (2026-10-04)

The user approved using PolyForm Noncommercial as the starting point with the
requested policy changes, following discussion of dropping source-sharing.
LICENSE is now Silicoase Noncommercial License, Version 2.0; README and the
package LicenseRef match it. Version 1.0 remains preserved in Git at 9a9f992.
The independent review of that version remains unchanged at 663e012.

The draft follows the template's acceptance, copyright/distribution/modification
grants, personal-use examples, patent defense, definitions and no-liability
structure. It removes the original name/URL from the operative license as required
by the template's modification terms. This file records provenance separately.

Changes from the template:
* Names Silicoase, Inc.; covers documentation and embedded assets; preserves
  separately licensed material and clarifies contributor-owned additions.
* Replaces the blanket nonprofit/education permission with use-based restrictions
  and a salary/stipend/non-industry-grant safe harbor for noncommercial research.
* Explicitly requires agreements for for-profit company uses, industry-sponsored
  research, commercial reuse and paid services, including cost recovery.
* Requires the actual custom license text with copies, rather than offering the
  original template URL as an alternative. Preserves notices and identifies changes.
* Adds Silicoase branding and output provisions. Modified interfaces may retain
  existing branding with a conspicuous modified/unofficial identification; removing
  interface branding is permitted while legal notices remain required.
* Clarifies first-violation reinstatement before notice or within 32 days of notice,
  later violations and complying downstream recipients.

Changes from MAD Version 1.0:
* Removes corresponding-source and network-source-disclosure duties. Forks may
  distribute executable copies without revealing changes, or host modified versions
  without source offers, provided their uses satisfy the restrictions. Original
  MAD material still carries its license; this does not allow commercial reuse.
* Allows separate terms for independently authored modifications that do not
  restrict recipients' rights in the underlying MAD code. Contributor grants apply
  when additions are distributed under this license; no assignment or automatic
  commercial relicensing right is acquired by Silicoase.
* Retains the template's express patent permission instead of Version 1.0's no-
  patent-license provision, limited to permitted purposes and the respective
  licensor/contributor contributions. This is a substantive change for counsel
  to review, not a statement that MAD has any applicable patents.

Counsel/provenance questions remain: actual ownership documentation, contractor
and external-contributor permissions, patent grant, branding provenance, tuition-
funded education at the boundary of paid teaching, and jurisdiction/enforcement.
No contribution agreement has been created, no publication has been performed,
and no ability to revoke previously granted permissions is assumed.

Generated report handling remains a practical release follow-up: README now
instructs sharers to include LICENSE with HTML reports. The exporter has not been
changed to embed the license automatically. A release can automate that notice
delivery without requiring disclosure of modifications.
